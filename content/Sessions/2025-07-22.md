## In the vision of [[SoulEarth]]

- As [[Merric]] finishes carving his path, the scales swing back to balance as if all weight had been lifted. 
	- The giant dog turns away from [[Merric]] with a whimper and a surge of anger washes over him from the obsidian greatsword as it begins to crack. 
	- The stone beneath this feet begins to crack and he dissolves into the ground. The ground shifts and is replaced by smooth dark, cold, timber as he finds himself back on the boat.
- The boat appears larger and fancier and more comfortable - feeling more like a boat designed to carry this many people.
	- [[Aust]] and [[Ultrin]] also notice that the ferryman’s demeanour has changed - he now looks backwards to the back of the boat, where his staff/oar is leaving a swirl of colours in the water, leaving something like the colour slick of an oil spill behind. [[The Ferryman]] seems transfixed on this.
	- [[Merric]] also notices that the river seems subtly different - the river is no longer a void but is now reflective and seems “closer”
- [[Merric]] finds himself still holding [[Jungo]]’s wooden sword - it feels different, as if its subtle warning magic is somehow different and perhaps stronger. He also feels a calmness about the gods and their grand designs - as if he sees through the illusion. ((Proficiency in religion)).
- [[Ultrin]] begins to feel a magical heat from the book that he carries. 
	- [[Ultrin]] whips it out in the middle of the boat.
	- The book hasn’t changed, other than being warm. It appears to be trying to open itself - which is new. [[Ultrin]] allows it to open. 
- [[Jorund]] hears the voice of (presumably) [[Dawnbringer]] in his mind - sounding sad. The voice says, ”The [[Raven Queen]] keeps a ledger and so does [[Urlogan]]. [[Merric]] no longer appears in either. It is a lonely fate for a soul.” 
- The book opens to a page [[Ultrin]] doesn’t recall (despite having read it all). The ink on the page shimmers like oil, but darker. There are tangled Abysal glyphs on the page and a sigil pressed into the page, indented, in the shape of a sword wrapped in chains.
	- [[Merric]] asks [[Ultrin]] about the book but is told he couldn’t read it
- [[Aust]] asks [[The Ferryman]] about the river and is told “I don’t understand. It should not be so. The fates cannot be altered and yet I felt it. Subtle, but you changed what the river was showing. You re-wrote a memory and thus…. This”
	- [[Ultrin]] and [[Merric]] notice the river is slightly reflective, like a mirror. [[Ultrin]] is concerned by the sigil in his book being a mirror and [[Merric]] is generally worried about mirrors.
	- [[Merric]] tries looking into the reflection of the river. He sees his own reflection.
	- [[Ultrin]] looks past the reflective surface to the pinpoints of light below. Everything under the surface looks about the same.
	- [[Jorund]] is Frightened by his reflection - it is many different faces, outfits, ages and species - they are all him in different disguises (some of which he recognises, but not all). Everyone sees him recoil from his reflection. [[Aust]] asks him if it was a fish.
- [[Ultrin]] looks at the colours from the back of the boat. He gets the feeling that the vibrant colours don’t really exist in the material plane. It reminds him of the Faewild, as if the planes were bleeding into the river. 
- [[Merric]] asks [[The Ferryman]] how he can tell that fates have been altered. “I can feel it”. [[Merric]] feels that he has nudged things one step closer to being resolved and remembers that he “changed” the past.
	- [[Merric]] asks [[The Ferryman]] if that was actually real, or just a vision. “Your souls are a ledger of your actions in life. Memories are as real as the present experience. Just temporally they are further away, but no less real.” 
- A sense of weightlessness, as if cresting a wave, is followed by a jolt, as if the boat lands. The boat begins to shudder as if running aground. 
	- For the first time we see a reaction of fear and confusion from [[The Ferryman]], who appears to be panicking and trying to fix the situation.
	- The boat begins to sink, as if going underwater. 
	- The world starts to reshape. The boat is gone and the river solidifies into smooth black stone. Columns of coloured glass rise into the sky like giant ribs.
	- The light filtering through the glass pillars is showing some kind of fleeting images - a young boy selling relics, a hooded figure passing a scroll, many shady deals. 
	- At the far end of the pillars is a cathedral made of mostly bone and some glass. [[Jorund]] feels a coin in his hand.
- [[Jorund]] feels like the images were familiar to him - memories of his past - which he announces to the group.
	- [[Aust]] asks where we’re headed - [[Merric]] asks what the worst day was in his life. [[Jorund]] can’t remember any particularly bad other than today. 
	- The bone cathedral doesn’t look to be for a specific religion. 
	- [[Jorund]] feels there’s nothing intrinsically evil about the bone cathedral.
- [[Jorund]] explains he used to do deals with various cults, including death cults. He sold them fake ”artefacts” of their gods. 
	- [[Ultrin]] is focused on the motive of selling things as the important bit, over the motive for purchasing.
- [[Jorund]] tells the group that one time the [[Raven Queen]] inquisition found him at the wrong party and he had to explain to them he wasn’t a cultist and was hired as a result.
- The group moves towards the [[Bone Cathedral]] and the door opens by itself as we approach. Inside it is huge, empty and silent. There are 5 towering stain glass windows that are cracked, misshapen and look almost animated and moving.
	- Standing in front of the closest window is a young human male boy dressed in white, barefoot, eyes dark and sunken, silently staring at [[Jorund]]. The boy is wearing a signet ring.
- [[Jorund]] says “hello” which echos throughout the cathedral. 
	- The boy speaks with a weight beyond his years, “hello”. When asked what he’s doing here “I was waiting for you. I thought you’d come alone. You built this place.”
	- [[Jorund]] doesn‘t remember building it, which doesn’t surprise the boy, who asks if we are friends and if we know who he is and what he did. We all look at the first window.
- The window depicts a younger [[Jorund]], wearing a cap, pedalling baubles that appear vaguely religious/restorative/protective. [[Jorund]] remembers this as a time he had to survive, and religion is all about giving people a false feeling of safety. There are several people exchanging coin for trinkets. 
	- A woman purchases a burnt offering, looks relieved and appears in later panels weeping over a fresh grave and throwing the trinket away (as if it were trash).
- The boy stares at the window. After a while he speaks: “You sold them dreams. They lit them like lanterns. The creatures of the night are attracted to the light.” Then he walks off to the second window.
	- [[Jorund]] and [[Merric]] don’t get any feeling of malice from the image, just desperate people turning to nonsense. Even if the baubles had been real, that would arguably be worse - if people were suspended on the brink of death. Instead people were given a brief moment of (illegitimate) hope.
-  At the second window is a depiction of a farmer, pleading as his crops wither. He looks to be starving and gives coin to a young [[Jorund]] who gives him a bone carved idol wrapped in bark. The farmer returns to his field, the sky crackling with lightning. The crops turn fully withered as a storm elemental ravages the field.
	- The boy intones, “they asked for rain. You gave them a storm.”
	- [[Aust]] thinks it looks like [[Jorund]] contributed to the destructive side of nature, without really intending to. 
	- [[Jorund]] remembers buying a cheap trinket and selling for lots - it had no real power, it was just bark. [[Ultrin]], [[Merric]] and [[Jorund]] remember that [[Talos]] priests will pose as a weather magicians to bring extremes of weather, inviting uncontrolled destruction from their god - too much rain, plus all the destruction it brings. 
- [[Ultrin]] asks how many trinkets [[Jorund]] sold that accidentally did good things. [[Jorund]] says that’s not his problem. He thinks he maybe had some repeat customers, but while in different disguises.
	- [[Merric]] points out that it’s [[Jorund]]’s fault, because if he hadn’t sold the trinket then the farmer then his farm wouldn’t have been destroyed.
- [[Ultrin]] moves to the next window with the child. He sees [[Jorund]] in a religious robe, handing a scroll to another hooded figure. Behind him is the [[House of the Moon]] (comparable to the [[House of Knowledge]] but in Baldures Gate). The ink from the scroll seems to spread from the scroll to other texts - tainting them. Clerics and mages scream at each other over a broken lecturn as the seeds of distrust lead to division. The original scroll unrolls to show a white jawless skull on a purple sunburst. 
	- As the others approach, [[Merric]] & [[Aust]] recognise the symbol of Syrric - whose domain is trickery, deception and driving wedges between people. He is difficult to study. His followers are known for embedding tainted ideologies into sects which then destroys them from the insides, wreaking havoc. 
	- The boy said: “The ink lied, perhaps you inspired it”
- The 4th window shows a slum chapel, many bodies piled up - sleeping, surviving. [[Jorund]] is visiting and appears to be meeting some high priest who hands over coin. [[Jorund]] has a black blook and is tallying up the names of the needy who are marked as blesséd in exchange for coin. [[Jorund]] exists the chapel and sells the ledger for further profit. The latter panels of the window show the chapel burning. On the wall is a depiction of a mace with a human skull where its head should be, dripping acid.
	- This is the symbol of [[Orcus]], who’s followers infiltrate and mark souls for [[Orcus]] to harvest. 
	- The boy said: “You wrote the names, he read them” 
- [[Merric]] mentions to [[Jorund]] that the [[Raven Queen]] is just self appointed so who cares what she thinks. [[Jorund]] tells [[Merric]] that [[Dawnbringer]] told him that he’s not in any ledger anymore. 
- The group heads to the 5th window shows [[Jorund]] in many disguises making many trades with many people. The last panel shows a boy - the boy - curled beneath an obsidian altar, his mouth sewn shut with hair, unconscious, with hooded figures moving around him, lighting black wax candles and drawing circles in a bone coloured powder
	- [[Jorund]] doesn’t remember the trades. They weren’t the best trades, compared to religious artefacts. He recognises the altar but not the boy. 
	- [[Ultrin]] thinks it looks like a reanimation ritual, with the boy as a sacrifice. He takes notes for later reference. He knows that for reanimation requires sacrificing a life that has more “Value” than other lives (powerful, magical, noble, etc) - the boy’s signet ring suggests he’s probably noble. [[Ultrin]] thinks the ring is the sigil of [[House Portier]] - one of the 4 houses of the ruling council of [[Baldure’s Gate]]
	- The boy speaks up: “You didn’t know. But, you didn’t ask.” The window begins to shudder and gains a sense of depth beyond a glass window. 
- The party find themselves drawn into the window - sitting in packed pews, halfway back and spread out, shoulder to shoulder with a large cult. The cultists are still and silent, staring forwards, hoods up. [[Jorund]] sits in the front row, just as he remembered - a guest of honour who was promised much coin, having supplied some reagents. 
	- [[Jorund]] remembers the ritual took place, there was much chaos and terror as it took hold and he deeply regretted **attending**.
	- This was the event when the Inquisition stormed in and his life took a turn.
